EmbeddingGemma 2 preprocessing: tessl::embedgemma2_pre against transformers 5.19's processors (Apple M5 Pro, CPU)

lanes   tessl = target/release/bench_embedgemma2_pre (src/bin/bench_embedgemma2_pre.rs), BENCH_ITERS=15 BENCH_WARMUP=3
        hf    = bench/embedgemma2_pre_hf.py (AutoProcessor of snapshot 914f7f89: the torchvision Gemma4ImageProcessor on a
                PIL image, Gemma4AudioFeatureExtractor on a float32 clip, EmbeddingGemma2VideoProcessor on decoded frames
                with fps and duration), same env; torch 2.14.1, torchvision 0.29.1, numpy 2.5.3, 6 torch threads
run     bench_paired --format workloads --rounds 8 --clean-env --out bench/results/embedgemma2_pre_m5pro.json tessl=... hf=...
        every lane a fresh process (min of 15 timed calls in it), order reversed on odd rounds. No --evidence: the drift gate
        was not used (no provenance block; every round's stdout, stderr and load are in the .lanes directory, not committed).
inputs  identical in both lanes: integer formulas (pixel(f, y, x, c), sample(i)); each row's input_crc (CRC-32 of the input
        bytes) is equal in the two lanes for every workload (checked in the lane outputs). Not timed: input construction.
        image_WxH is one RGB image; audio_Ns N seconds of 16 kHz PCM; video_40f_WxH 40 decoded frames at 1 fps, which both
        sample to 32 and process at 140 soft tokens per frame (the per-frame figures below divide by 32).
threads each lane uses its own default: tessl up to 8 scoped threads on the resize and the FFT front end (one per 24 audio
        frames), transformers torch's intra-op pool and numpy

CAVEAT  the host was shared the whole time: 1-minute load average 72 to 146 on 18 cores while this ran (the 16 lane runs'
        own readings). Min-of-15 inside a process and interleaving of the lanes are what keep the ratios usable; the spread
        of single rounds is the measure of how much, and is printed. Both lanes were hit alike but not equally in every round.

RESULT  tessl is faster in every workload by its median round ratio. The one range that includes 1 is audio_1s: 0.14x to
        2.34x over the rounds (one tessl round was 1.54 ms against its usual 0.15 to 0.28 ms), so a 1 s clip, where both
        take about 0.2 ms, is not distinguished by this run. Elsewhere the lowest round is above 1: 1.4x to 7.4x.

                workload               tessl ms                  hf ms   hf / tessl, median [lowest, highest round]
           image_224x224                  0.746                  2.258   3.32x [2.02, 4.52]
           image_640x480                  1.153                  3.098   2.72x [1.72, 4.94]
         image_1920x1080                  2.643                  6.528   2.68x [1.43, 3.63]
         image_4000x3000                  4.767                 21.722   4.62x [2.70, 7.19]

               audio_10s                  0.853                  2.592   3.02x [2.09, 3.56]

       video_40f_640x360                 29.201                 80.869   2.38x [1.64, 6.35]

(median over 8 rounds of each round's min of 15; the ratio column is the median of the 8 per-round ratios.)

Per unit, tessl, median of round minima: 0.75 to 4.8 ms per image (224x224 to 4000x3000); 0.085 ms per second of audio for
a 10 s clip (0.079 for 30 s); 0.91 ms per sampled frame at 640x360 and 1.7 ms at 1280x720 (29.2 / 32 and 55.0 / 32).

What this does not show: single-thread speed (both lanes are multi-threaded; neither was pinned), anything on x86, or the
processors' cost with other kernels running on the same cores in a serving process. A speedup over transformers is the
claim, bounded by the spread above; no absolute number should be quoted from a shared host.
