Row reductions, simdgroup-first — M5 Pro, 2026-09-05
=====================================================

Kernels: softmax_rows_f32, row_sum_f32, row_max_f32 (reduce.metal) and the
rms_norm family (rms_norm.metal), all one threadgroup per row. Before: the
per-row reduction was a threadgroup-memory tree with a barrier per round
(REDUCE_TREE, ten rounds at 1024 lanes). After: `reduce_row_add` /
`reduce_row_max` in reduce_tree.h — simd_sum/simd_max across each simdgroup,
one lane per simdgroup publishes its partial, one barrier, then every
simdgroup folds the partials with a second shuffle. Rows are launched with at
most 256 lanes (REDUCE_ROW_MAX_LANES, was 1024).

Method: paired, order-balanced, in-process A/B with the previous kernels
kept under temporary names; twenty dispatches per command buffer, eight
calls per arm per round (per-call median), six rounds alternating order,
median of rounds. Only the paired ratios are the finding.

Two intermediate results that shaped the design (rows = 4096):

1. A first cut folded the simdgroup partials with a serial loop per thread.
   It won below 256 columns and LOST from 1024 up (softmax 0.51x, row_sum
   0.69x, rms 0.74–0.94x): 32 dependent threadgroup-memory loads per thread
   cost more than the tree's ten barriers. The second-level shuffle removed
   the loop.
2. A register-resident single-pass softmax (rows up to 8 * tptg read once)
   never beat the three-pass streaming form (0.51–1.05x): the re-reads are
   cache hits; the cost was the reduction, not row traffic. Dropped.
